4 Glass vs. contrast — the decision, and the onboarding palette carried into the list
THE DECISION
Glass belongs to the chrome, not to the canvas. Keep both.

These two aren't in tension — they're two different layers that the list screens currently merge. In your onboarding, the canvas is flat near-black and every element sits on it at full strength. In the list screens, the blue wash is painted behind the content, so a row's legibility depends on how far down the screen it happens to be.

Measured on your own tokens: text.secondary #7d8590 scores 5.0:1 against #0d1117 at the top of the list and 3.3:1 against the #1d3550 the wash reaches at the bottom. Same component, same colour, failing or passing by scroll position. That is the actual bug — not glass.

And it's what Liquid Glass is for: Apple's material is a property of floating navigation and controls, with opaque content passing underneath. Applying it to the page background is the one use it isn't designed for. So: flat near-black canvas (your onboarding's), opaque content cards, and glass reserved for the header, the Filter sheet, and the action pill. Contrast becomes constant, and the glass gets more visible because things now move under it.

SURFACE RULES
Canvas bg.primary #0d1117 — the token you already ship, just flat: no gradient, no wash. That alone is the contrast fix.
Content card bg.secondary #161b22, 1px blue-tinted hairline — opaque, never translucent. Using bg.secondary rather than bg.card keeps cards close to the canvas while still separating; secondary text lands at 5.5:1 here, at every scroll position. No new tokens in any of this.
Glass chrome — header, Filter sheet, action pill. Apple's native GlassView glassEffectStyle="regular" on iOS 26+, BlurView intensity 40 elsewhere — your existing GlassView, unchanged. (The CSS here only approximates it.)
Glow — only under the primary CTA and behind the brand mark, exactly as onboarding uses it. Never behind body text.
THE NON-APPLE PATH

On Android, and on iOS under Reduce Transparency, the three glass surfaces swap to opaque #0d1117 with the same hairline. No layout, type or spacing changes — glass is one fill, so it degrades to a colour rather than to a different design. Your GlassView already does all three branches — native material, BlurView, and an opaque bg.secondary under Reduce Transparency. The one change this proposal needs is upstream of it: Card routes to GlassCard on glass themes, so every content card is currently a material layer. Cards go opaque; the header, sheet and pill keep the material. (GlassFill is already inert — it returns null unless material is passed, and nothing passes it.)

IF YOU WANT ONBOARDING'S BLACKER CANVAS

Onboarding reads darker than #0d1117 — closer to #08090c. Going there is a deliberate re-ramp of bg.* across all fifteen themes, with the same cost I argue against for accents in 3b — and it buys roughly 0.3:1. The gradient removal buys 1.7:1. Do the free one first; treat the darker ramp as a separate, later call.

Mono eyebrows (> 01 / LANGUAGE becomes NEEDS YOU · 1), blue-tinted card hairlines, the glow under the filled CTA — all three are in 4a. One more thing they settle for free: onboarding already uses green for "connected / live" ("HANDSHAKE COMPLETE", "Thread is live."). That is the product telling you green already means alive — which is the argument for 3b-A that I didn't have yesterday.
